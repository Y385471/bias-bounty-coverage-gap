Best Bias Discovery: homes missing from both maps are scored as "fully mapped"

The pattern. Every scorecard metric compares Overture with Microsoft footprints. When both layers miss the same homes they agree, so building_gap = 0. Even the raw, unclipped disagreement is 0. Only a count of homes that is independent of both maps can reveal this.

The independent benchmark. I used Census ACS 2019–2023 table B25024, units in structure, to count expected residential structures per tract:

detached + attached/2 + 2-unit/2 + (3–4)/3.5 + (5–9)/7 + (10–19)/14.5 + (20–49)/35 + (50+)/100 + mobile homes + boats/RVs

The median is 1.21 Microsoft footprints per structure. That is above 1 because footprints also include garages, sheds and shops, so a threshold of 0.5 is conservative.

Result. 78 tracts, home to 306,821 people, hold fewer than 0.5 Microsoft footprints per residential structure. They are not new construction. ACS B25034 puts 4–6 % of their homes after 2010, against 9 % elsewhere. The machine-learning reference simply missed them.

  • Patched by volunteers (47 tracts, 193k people). Overture has these homes, and 94 % of its buildings there come from OpenStreetMap (57,479 of 61,126). Median SVI 0.51, poverty (<150 % FPL) 14.9 %, no vehicle 3.3 %.
  • Not patched (31 tracts, 114k people). Median SVI 0.69–0.76, poverty 25–38 %, no vehicle 6.6–13.7 %, limited English up to 7.1 %.
    • Of these, 16 tracts are a double miss: Overture holds fewer than 0.5 footprints per structure too.
    • 18 of the 31 are in Harris County (Houston), against 8 of the 47 patched tracts.
  • The scored building_gap is 0 in 71 of the 78 hole tracts. The scorecard reports them as fully mapped.

Named tracts.

  • 48201550404 (Harris, TX): 1,714 detached homes in ACS, Microsoft 165, Overture 175, SVI 0.69, gap 0. The satellite figure in the write-up shows a complete subdivision missing from both layers, while the streets across the tract line are fully mapped.
  • 48201551000 (Harris, TX): 992 detached homes, Microsoft 95, Overture 102, SVI 0.81, gap 0.
  • 04013422218 (Maricopa, AZ, patched): 1,090 detached homes, Microsoft 1, Overture 1,065.
  • 04013103305 (Maricopa, AZ): SVI 0.98, 37 % mobile homes, Microsoft 0. The building component is undefined, so the tract drops out of the building score entirely.

Robustness. I ran 18 variants: three structure weightings × hole thresholds 0.4 / 0.5 / 0.6 × patched cut-offs 0.7 / 0.8, each with a permutation test on median SVI (5,000 shuffles).

  • The no-vehicle share is higher in unpatched holes in 18 of 18 variants, and poverty in 17 of 18.
  • The SVI difference is significant at threshold 0.6 under every weighting (p = 0.001–0.036).
  • At 0.5 it is mixed.
  • At 0.4 it is not significant. The most extreme holes are high-SVI whether patched or not.

So the claim is stated in two parts: reference holes concentrate in vulnerable urban tracts, and among moderate holes, volunteers patched the less deprived ones.

Why it matters.

  • Footprints are the base layer for post-hurricane and post-flood remote damage assessment. A tract missing about 90 % of its homes reports about 90 % fewer affected structures, in tracts where more than a quarter of households are below 150 % of the poverty line.
  • Shelter demand and door-to-door evacuation notices are planned from structure counts, and up to 13.7 % of these households have no car.
  • Rooftop geocoding falls back to street-range interpolation when a building is missing.
  • Harris County has been hit by Harvey (2017), Beryl (2024) and repeated floods.

Related work. Discussion 34844 shows that clipping hides reference surplus on tribal land. This is the complementary blind spot, a shared deficit, and only an outside benchmark can see it.

Methodology (Best Documentation)

  • Pipeline. One file, coverage_gap.py, with DuckDB 1.5.4 reading straight from the bucket (s3_url_style='path', because the bucket name has dots). It runs in about 20 minutes on a free Colab CPU and writes the submission plus every per-tract count.
  • Transport. Named-highway length clipped to each tract with ST_Intersection, measured in EPSG:5070 with always_xy := true; without it, DuckDB reads the CRS84 layers as lat/lon and returns inf. Overture motorway / trunk / primary / secondary against TIGER S1100 / S1200.
  • Buildings. Counts with bounding-box-centre attachment.
  • POI. The mean of two halves. The facilities half averages the defined per-type gaps for fire, EMS and schools, matched on categories.primary, with hospitals excluded. The other half is all Overture places against cbp_estab.
  • Composite. The mean of the defined components only.
  • Edge cases.
    • Undefined is a property of the reference side: written as 0 with its flag FALSE and excluded from the mean.
    • No Overture data with a reference present gives a full gap of 1.
    • Zero-population tracts are scored normally.
    • The 99xx water tracts are not in the scored list.
    • GEOID is always text.
    • The scorer reads the 2nd column, so the file starts with GEOID, coverage_gap_score.
    • pandas / urllib gets a 403 from the proxy, so CSVs are read through DuckDB.
  • Checks.
    • Transport-undefined counts match the README exactly: 218 / 869 / 253 / 1,704.
    • Leaderboard RMSE is 0.000145 under both centroid and bbox-centre attachment; 47 tracts change between them. The residual is reported as open, not tuned away.

Limitations are in the write-up: ACS sampling error, so tracts under 150 structures are excluded; assumed structure weights, bracketed by the robustness grid; and non-residential footprints.
